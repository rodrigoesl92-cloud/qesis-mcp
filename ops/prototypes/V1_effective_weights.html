An index that declares five weights and behaves as if it has two.

Nominal weight is what the composite says it multiplies each axis by. Realised weight is the first-order sensitivity index measured from the same 32 states. They disagree. Water stress realises roughly 1.8 times its declared weight, and it is the only axis whose nominal value falls outside its own confidence interval.

Nominal weight (declared) Realised main effect Si Nominal outside its own interval 95% bootstrap interval, 4000 resamples
Spearman 0.9329

Deleting ODI, RGD and REE entirely, which is 40% of the nominal weight, leaves a ranking that correlates with the published one at this level. The composite is reported as five-dimensional and orders states as if it were two-dimensional.

What this view is not licensed to say. At n=32 the bootstrap intervals are wide. Only WSE's inflation is distinguishable from its nominal weight at 95%. The point estimates for ODI, RGD and REE are near zero but their intervals do not exclude the nominal values. The defensible claim is that nominal weights are not identified at this sample size, not that those three axes are weightless.

Falsifier. A sample large enough to tighten the ODI, RGD and REE intervals away from zero, or a re-estimation in which WSE's nominal 0.30 returns inside its interval. Either outcome retires this finding.
